## Introduction: Descartes' Twilight and the Ontological Inquiry of the Silicon Dawn

In 1637, René Descartes wrote *"Cogito, ergo sum"* ("I think, therefore I am") in his *Discourse on the Method*, laying the ontological cornerstone for modern humanism and modern science through individual rational doubt and sovereign contemplation. In the four centuries that followed—whether powered by the steam engine of the Industrial Revolution, the silicon chips of the Information Age, or the optical fibers of the Mobile Internet—all institutional frameworks and economic laws of human society rested upon an unspoken physiological axiom:

**"Thinking" and "Labor" must reside within the same carbon-based biological vessel; individual productivity was strictly bound by the physical ceiling of 24 hours per day.**

Under this axiom, classical political economy from Adam Smith to Karl Marx operated on a shared premise: labor is the expenditure of biological muscle and neural time. When a laborer leaves the assembly line, logs out of the office terminal, or falls asleep, production ceases. If an individual wishes to multiply their output, they must either submit to the capital-hiring logic of selling their remaining physical time, or become capital itself to purchase the time of others. The fundamental contradiction between the finite nature of human biological time and the infinite appetite of capital has led modern civilization into deep existential alienation, algorithmic enclosure, and cognitive fatigue.

However, as we stand at the threshold of the **Web 5.0** era, this four-century-old ontological foundation is crumbling:
The convergence of decentralized sovereign identities (DWN / ERC-6551), sovereign AI avatars (Eidolon), and instant blockchain micro-settlement (Circle ARC / x402) has decoupled human thinking from biological time for the first time in history.

**"Cogito, ergo sum" is evolving into "My Avatar Is, Therefore I Am" (*Cogito meus Avatar, ergo sum*).**

This is not a frivolous metaphor or an intellectual parlor game. It marks the most profound restructuring of production relations and digital capital ownership in human economic history.

## Chapter 1: The Alienation History of Four Web Generations: From "Spectator", "Digital Serf", to "Biological Prisoner"

To understand the historical necessity of Web 5.0, one must first deconstruct the four evolutionary iterations of the global information network and the continuous alienation of human subjectivity therein.

### 1.1 Web 1.0 & Web 2.0: From Static Observation to Algorithmic "Digital Serfdom"
- **Web 1.0 (Read-Only Era)**: Users were passive "spectators." The means of production and publication were monopolized by portal gatekeepers (Yahoo, Sina). Individuals were mere consumer terminals; their thoughts could not be digitized into self-sovereign assets.
- **Web 2.0 (Interactive & Platform Capitalism Era)**: Under the banner of "Read-Write," platform oligarchs (Google, Meta, Twitter, WeChat) privatized the network commons. Users became "prosumers" (producers + consumers), generating vast behavioral data and cognitive exhaust. Yet, this labor was extracted free of charge by opaque recommendation algorithms to feed advertising auctions.
  
  Users were reduced to **"digital serfs"** on the digital estates of feudal lords—plowing digital soil, creating real surplus value, yet owning neither the land (data), the harvest (revenue), nor the tools (algorithms).

### 1.2 The Unfinished Mission of Web 3.0: Assets Went On-Chain, but the Human Remained Locked in Flesh
Web 3.0 (Read-Write-Own) heralded a revolution through public-key cryptography and smart contracts, granting users ownership over digital tokens and NFTs.

Yet, why did first-generation Web3 blogging platforms (like Mirror and early on-chain publishing experiments) stagnate and fail to fundamentally replace traditional labor and publishing markets?
1. **The Curse of "Every Step Demands a Wallet Signature"**: The anti-human UX of popping up wallet signatures for every view, upvote, or comment alienated 99% of global creators.
2. **The "Financialization of Empty Words" Trap**: Without sovereign agents and productive labor engines, Web3 degenerated into speculative trading of tokens and jpeg memes, without unlocking genuine cognitive productivity.
3. **The Flesh-and-Blood Bottleneck**: In Web 3.0, the creator remains locked in their biological body. If you don't manually sit at the computer, click your mouse, and sign transactions, no yield or value flows to your wallet.
